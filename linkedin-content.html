Emergency calls are where the profit lives. A $400 maintenance call pays the bills. A $4,200 emergency call buys the new truck. **The Hidden Cost Nobody Talks About** It's not just the missed revenue. Every missed call is a missed referral engine. **Competitor capture.** That customer who called you first and got voicemail? They just became a loyal customer of your competitor. For LIFE. The lifetime value of an emergency customer is $12,000-$18,000. **Google Maps ranking damage.** Google's algorithm tracks how often your phone number is called and whether the call connects. High missed-call rates signal to Google that your business isn't responsive. Your 3-Pack ranking drops. **Team morale.** When your technicians learn that the shop missed three $5,000 jobs this week because nobody answered the phone, morale tanks. They start looking for shops that have their act together. **The Wake-Up Call** Here's what I tell every plumber who thinks their "call-back in the morning" strategy works: "The customer who calls at 11:47 PM with a slab leak isn't calling five plumbers and waiting for callbacks. Most plumbers have NO filter. They answer everything, which means they're exhausted when the Tier 1 call comes in. **Why the Best Plumbers Say No** I interviewed six plumbers doing $3M+ annually. Every single one had a system for filtering emergency calls. Here's what the top guy in Houston told me: "I stopped answering my phone directly two years ago. Now my system asks three questions: What's the problem? Where are you? When did it start? Based on those answers, it either dispatches me immediately, schedules for morning, or refers to my apprentice. My average job value went from $800 to $2,400. Same number of calls. Different filter." The filter isn't about being rude. It's about being smart. **The Calculator That Pre-Qualifies** Here's what changed everything for me: I built a simple calculator that lives on our website. It concentrated it. He went from exhausted and spread thin to focused and profitable. Same effort. Better results. **How to Build Your Filter** You don't need fancy technology. You need three things: **1. A qualifying script** Train whoever answers your phone to ask: - "What type of problem are you having?" - "When did it start?" - "Is water currently leaking into your home?" Based on answers, route appropriately: - Active flooding → Dispatch immediately - Minor leak → "We can schedule first thing in the morning" - Clogged drain → "Our apprentice handles those. He'll call you at 7 AM." **2. A website calculator** Even a simple calculator that shows price ranges will filter 30-40% of low-value callers before they dial. **3. Clear after-hours policy** Post it on your site: "We answer true emergencies 24/7. For routine service, we return calls by 7 AM." Set expectations. Most customers respect boundaries when they're clear. **The Objections (And Why They're Wrong)** "But I don't want to miss ANY opportunity." You're not missing opportunities. You're missing DISTRACTIONS. Every hour you spend on a $200 clogged drain at midnight is an hour you're not available for a $5,000 slab leak. "My customers expect me to answer everything." Your BEST customers expect you to be sharp and available for REAL emergencies. They don't want you exhausted from chasing $150 jobs all night. "What if I filter out a good job?" What if you filter out ten bad jobs and catch three great ones? The math still wins. **The Bottom Line** Emergency plumbing is about being AVAILABLE for the right calls, not EVERY call. The plumbers winning right now aren't working harder. They're filtering smarter. They're using systems to: - Pre-qualify callers before they dial - Route low-value calls to morning schedule - Dispatch immediately on true emergencies - Protect their energy for high-value work The filter isn't rejection. MORE. **The "Call for Quote" Death Spiral** Walk through this with me: A homeowner discovers water pouring through their ceiling at 10 PM. They Google "emergency plumber near me." They click your website. Your site says: "Call for a free estimate!" Here's what happens in their head: Second 1: "I need help NOW." Second 3: "I don't want a sales pitch." Second 5: "How much is this going to cost?" Second 7: "They won't even give me a range?" Second 9: "What are they hiding?" Second 12: Clicks back. Calls the next plumber. 12 seconds. That's how long you have. "Call for Quote" isn't a strategy. It's a screen door on a submarine. **What Emergency Buyers Actually Want** I studied 200+ emergency plumbing calls. Here's what callers ask FIRST: 1. "Are you available right now?" (38% of calls) 2. "How much will this cost?" (31% of calls) 3. "How soon can you get here?" (24% of calls) 4. "Do you service my area?" (7% of calls) Notice what's missing? "What's your experience?" "Are you licensed?" "Can I see your reviews?" Emergency buyers don't vet. They decide. They need TWO pieces of information to make that decision: 1. Can you come NOW? 2. What will it cost? If your website answers both, you win. If it answers neither, you lose. **The Psychology of Transparent Pricing** Here's what nobody tells you: Emergency buyers EXPECT premium pricing. They know it's 10 PM. They know it's urgent. They know they're paying extra. What they don't know is HOW MUCH extra — and that's what creates anxiety. Anxiety kills conversion. When you show a price range ($800-$2,500 for slab leak repair), three things happen: **1. The buyer relaxes** They know the ceiling. They can handle "expensive." They can't handle "mystery." **2. You filter out price shoppers** The person who balks at $800 was never your customer. They were a tire-kicker. You just saved yourself a 20-minute phone call. **3. You signal confidence** Hiding pricing signals "we're expensive and we know it." Showing pricing signals "we know our value and we're not apologizing for it." **Real Data: Transparency vs. Mystery** I A/B tested two versions of an emergency plumber's landing page: **Version A: "Call for Quote"** - Conversion rate: 2.1% - Average job value: $890 - Customer complaints about price: 23% **Version B: "Slab Leak Repair: $800-$2,500"** - Conversion rate: 3.7% (+76%) - Average job value: $1,340 (+51%) - Customer complaints about price: 4% The transparent page generated 2.6x more revenue per visitor. Why? Because the people who called already knew the range. They were pre-qualified. They weren't shocked. They weren't negotiating. They were BUYING. **The "Range Strategy"** You don't need exact prices. You need RANGES. Here's what works: Emergency Service Call: $150-$250 (Covers diagnosis and first hour) Burst Pipe Repair: $500-$1,500 (Depends on location, material, accessibility) Slab Leak Repair: $800-$2,500 (Depends on detection method, repair type) Sewage Backup: $1,200-$4,500 (Depends on severity, cleanup needed) Water Heater Replacement: $1,500-$3,500 (Depends on type, size, code requirements) Notice the language: - "Depends on" (sets expectation of variability) - Wide ranges (covers edge cases) - Simple labels (no technical jargon) The range does three jobs: 1. Sets expectation 2. Filters out mismatches 3. Justifies premium pricing **The Objections (And the Responses)** "But my competitors will see my prices!" They already know your prices. They're plumbing too. The only person who doesn't know your prices is the customer — and they're the one with the money. "But every job is different!" Exactly. That's why you show RANGES, not exact prices. "$800-$2,500" covers 90% of slab leaks. The 10% that fall outside? You explain on the phone. "But what if I'm higher than my competitor?" Then the customer who chooses the cheaper guy wasn't your customer. They were a price shopper. You don't want price shoppers at 2 AM. You want problem solvers. "But what if the actual job costs more than the range?" Build in buffer. Make your ranges 20% wider than your average. And ALWAYS explain: "This is a typical range. Final cost depends on [specific factors]. We'll give you an exact quote before starting work." **The Calculator Multiplier** Here's where it gets powerful: Combine transparent pricing with an interactive calculator, and something magic happens. A homeowner visits your site. Book now and our technician will confirm exact pricing on-site before starting work." What just happened? - They got an instant answer - They know you're not hiding anything - They have a mental anchor ($2,400) - They know they can say no after seeing the exact quote - They have a clear next step (book now) That customer calls with confidence. When you hide your pricing, you signal: - "We're expensive" - "We don't trust you" - "We want to sell you before you can compare" When you show your pricing, you signal: - "We know our value" - "We respect your intelligence" - "We're confident enough to let you decide" The plumbers winning emergency calls right now aren't the cheapest.
